How much of the whale short is a bet?

November 2026 · Data from 2026-11-01 01:44 to 2026-11-02 15:45 UTC

"Whales are short BTC" gets posted every week. I wanted to know how much of that short is a view on price. A wallet that shorts $50M of HYPE while staking $50M of HYPE has no view at all, yet the raw long/short count still files it as a short.

Live dashboard Code and full method

What I found

Two thirds of the large HYPE shorts are hedges. Wallets hold spot or staked HYPE against $491M of the $772M in whale-size HYPE shorts. They earn staking yield and carry no price risk. Take those out and the $1M+ accounts go from 51% short to 31%. The traders with real exposure lean long.
Market makers don't explain the BTC short. I removed market makers, vaults, relative-value books and on-chain hedges, and the $1M+ group moved from 62% to 65% short. Market makers hold more BTC long than short, so dropping them pushes the share up. The largest BTC shorts trade 1 to 4 times their account per month and hold no BTC on Hyperliquid. They could be betting on a drop, or hedging BTC held on an exchange or in an ETF. On-chain data can't tell those apart.
The biggest accounts drive the headline. Across every tracked wallet with a $50k+ BTC position, 54% of the notional is short. Among accounts above $1M it is 62%. At the top, ETH sits at 51% and SOL at 56%.
Positioning barely moved. Over a day and a half each coin's short share stayed inside a band of about 4 points (BTC 58.7% to 62.2%). A level this stable says little on its own. I expect changes in notional, and behaviour on volatile days, to carry more signal.

How I measured it

Finding the wallets

Hyperliquid has no endpoint that lists every position. I start from the public leaderboard (about 48k addresses) and add any address that trades $50k or more in a day on the live BTC, ETH, SOL and HYPE trade stream. In its first hour the trade stream found 33 accounts above $1M that the leaderboard never listed.

About 17k wallets are in the set. The ~275 accounts above $1M get polled every 15 minutes and drive the charts above. The rest rotate. Tracked positions cover 41–47% of open interest, depending on the coin.

Labelling them

LabelRule
Vaultaddress is a Hyperliquid vault
Market maker30-day volume ≥ 100× account value
Relative value|net| / gross exposure across all coins < 0.3
Directionaleverything else

For each short I also check the wallet's spot and staked balance in the same asset. The covered part counts as hedged and comes off the directional short. Labels get stamped into each snapshot, so a wallet that changes behaviour next month doesn't rewrite this month's chart.

Do the cut-offs drive the result?

I picked the 100× and 0.3 thresholds by hand, because turnover has no natural gap between market makers and active traders. So I reran the numbers across 24 combinations, from 30× to no market-maker filter and from 0.2 to no relative-value filter:

BTC59.2%57.6% to 62.0%
ETH49.0%47.3% to 50.2%

No combination flips a conclusion. (Snapshot of 2026-11-02 morning, so the raw figures differ a little from the ones above.)

What this can't see

  • Hedges held off Hyperliquid. "Unhedged" means unhedged on Hyperliquid.
  • Old positions in wallets that are neither on the leaderboard nor trading. That gap is why coverage sits below half.
  • Who owns which wallet. One fund can run several addresses.

All of this comes from a day and a half of a quiet market. Read it as a description of the sample, and don't trade on it.
