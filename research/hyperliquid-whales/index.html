How much of the whale short is a bet?

November 2026 · Data from 2026-11-01 02:10 to 2026-11-13 12:30 UTC

"Whales are short BTC" gets posted every week. I wanted to know how much of that short is a view on price. A wallet that shorts $50M of HYPE while staking $50M of HYPE has no view at all, yet the raw long/short count still files it as a short.

Terms used here
Whale
Here, an account worth $1M or more on Hyperliquid.
Notional
Position size times price, in dollars.
Short share
Short notional divided by long plus short notional. Above 50% means more short than long.
Raw / unhedged
Raw counts every position. Unhedged drops market makers, vaults, relative-value wallets, and the part of a short covered by spot or staked coins in the same wallet.
Market maker
Here, a wallet that trades 100 times its account value or more in 30 days.
Vault
A pooled account on Hyperliquid that a manager trades for depositors.
Relative value
A wallet whose longs and shorts across all coins nearly offset (|net| / gross below 0.3).
Directional
Every wallet not in the three groups above.
Staking
Locking HYPE to help secure the network in exchange for a yield.
Open interest
The total size of open positions in a coin.
Coverage
Tracked notional as a share of both sides of open interest.
bps
Basis points. 100 bps = 1%.
p-value
How often random timestamps produce a result at least this strong. Small means chance is an unlikely explanation.

What I found

Two thirds of the large HYPE shorts are hedges. Wallets hold spot or staked HYPE against $500M of the $713M in whale-size HYPE shorts. They earn staking yield and carry no price risk. Take those out and the $1M+ accounts go from 51% short to 26%. The traders with real exposure lean long.
Market makers don't explain the BTC short. With market makers, vaults, relative-value books and on-chain hedges removed, the $1M+ group stayed between 58% and 70% short over the twelve days, and sits at 60% in the latest snapshot. Market makers swing between sides. Most days they held more BTC long than short, so removing them pushed the share up. On 5 and 6 November and again on 13 November they flipped short ($192M short against $36M long in the latest snapshot), which is why the raw share of 63% now sits above the unhedged one. The largest directional BTC shorts hold no BTC on Hyperliquid. They could be betting on a drop, or hedging BTC held on an exchange or in an ETF. On-chain data can't tell those apart.
The biggest accounts drive the headline. Across every tracked wallet with a $50k+ BTC position, 53% of the notional is short. Among accounts above $1M it is 63%. At the top, ETH sits at 59% and SOL at 61%.
The big accounts shorted into the sell-off. All four coins fell over the twelve days: BTC 11%, ETH 14%, SOL 16% and HYPE 16%. ETH went from 48% short to 59% among $1M+ accounts, and HYPE from 47% to 51%. BTC moved inside a 9-point band (55.5% to 64.7%). A falling price doesn't move the share by itself, because longs and shorts in the same coin reprice together, so these changes come from trading.
Wallets with a track record take stronger sides. Among directional accounts that made $1M or more before the last 30 days, 64% of BTC exposure is short and 90% of SOL, against 60% and 58% for all directional $1M+ accounts. In HYPE they sit at 17% short, even more long than everyone else. ETH is the exception: 59%, a little less short than the wider group's 62%. I keep the last 30 days out of the pick on purpose: a filter on recent profit just selects whoever sat on the right side of the latest move.

How I measured it

Finding the wallets

Hyperliquid has no endpoint that lists every position. I start from the public leaderboard (about 48k addresses) and add any address that trades $50k or more in a day on the live BTC, ETH, SOL and HYPE trade stream. In its first hour the trade stream found 33 accounts above $1M that the leaderboard never listed.

About 28k wallets are in the set. The ~280 accounts above $1M get polled every 15 minutes and drive the charts above. The rest rotate. Tracked positions cover 42–45% of open interest, depending on the coin.

Labelling them

For each short I also check the wallet's spot and staked balance in the same asset. The covered part counts as hedged and comes off the directional short. Labels get stamped into each snapshot, so a wallet that changes behaviour next month doesn't rewrite this month's chart.

Do the cut-offs drive the result?

I picked the 100× and 0.3 thresholds by hand, because turnover has no natural gap between market makers and active traders. So I reran the numbers across 24 combinations, from 30× to no market-maker filter and from 0.2 to no relative-value filter:

CoinRaw short shareUnhedged, all 24 combinations
BTC63.5%58.3% to 64.4%
ETH59.0%58.1% to 65.1%

Every combination leaves BTC, ETH and SOL short-heavy and HYPE long-heavy. Whether BTC's unhedged share lands above or below the raw one depends on the market-maker cut, which fits market makers that swing between sides. (Same snapshot as the numbers above, 2026-11-13 12:30 UTC.)

What this can't see

  • Hedges held off Hyperliquid. "Unhedged" means unhedged on Hyperliquid.
  • Old positions in wallets that are neither on the leaderboard nor trading. That gap is why coverage sits below half.
  • Who owns which wallet. One fund can run several addresses.

All of this comes from twelve days in which every coin fell. Read it as a description of that sample, and don't trade on it.

What's next

The level of positioning says little. The open question is whether changes in it lead price. Since November 2026 the pipeline logs every sharp shift in the $1M+ group, every $10M+ trade by a large account, and the positioning of a cohort that has made money over time, stamped as it was known at that moment. Once enough of these events pile up I will test whether price follows them more often than chance, and publish the result here either way.

After twelve days, one signal stands out. In the hour after a $1M+ account changed a position by $10M or more, price moved 54 bps in the direction of that change on average (35 events, p < 0.001 against random timestamps with the same mix of directions), and the move held over 4 and 24 hours. Shifts in the group's short share and in the profitable cohort showed nothing. I don't count this as a finding yet: the sample covers one falling market, several trades often land at the same moment, and I tested twelve signal and horizon pairs. I'll rerun it once each signal has a few hundred events.
