Do Hyperliquid longs still pay more than Binance longs?

November 2026 · Funding data from May 2023 to November 2026

Perpetual futures use funding, a payment between longs and shorts, to stay close to spot. Hyperliquid settles it every hour, Binance and Bybit every 8 hours. If one venue charges longs more for weeks on end, you can short there, go long on the other venue, stay flat on price and collect the difference. I measured how big that difference has been for BTC, ETH, SOL and HYPE, how long it lasts, and how much of it survives fees and margin.

Terms used here
Perp
Perpetual future: a futures contract with no expiry. Funding keeps its price close to spot.
Funding
A periodic payment between longs and shorts. When the perp trades above spot, longs pay shorts. Quoted here as % a year.
Interest term
The fixed part every venue adds to funding, 0.01% per 8 hours (about 11% a year). It cancels in the comparison.
Gap
Hyperliquid funding minus Binance or Bybit funding for the same coin and hour, annualised.
Legs
The two positions of the trade: short the perp on the venue that charges longs more, long the same coin on the other.
CEX
Centralised exchange, here Binance and Bybit.
bps
Basis points. 100 bps = 1%.
Taker / maker
A taker order fills at once against the book and pays the higher fee. A maker order rests in the book and pays less.
Slippage
How much worse than the best quote an order of a given size fills, because it eats through several price levels.
Half-life
How long it takes a gap to lose half of its distance from its normal level.
95% interval
The range of values for the true average that the data supports, at 95% confidence.
Block bootstrap
Resampling the data in 30-day chunks instead of single days, so the intervals keep the fact that the gap stays high or low for weeks.
Mark price
The price a venue uses to value positions and trigger liquidations.
T-bill
3-month US Treasury bill, the usual risk-free benchmark.

What I found

Hyperliquid longs have paid more, and the interest term doesn't explain it. All three venues add a fixed 0.01% per 8 hours to funding, about 11% a year. Hyperliquid longs pay it at rest, and so do Binance and Bybit longs, so it cancels when you compare them. What is left is a gap of 6.6% a year for BTC, 6.7% for ETH and 7.0% for SOL against Binance over the whole span. Bybit gives similar numbers for BTC and ETH (6.4% and 6.7%), lower for SOL (5.8%) and higher for HYPE.
The gap is closing. In 2024 it averaged 12% a year for BTC and 14.5% for SOL. In 2026 BTC sits at 2.3% (95% interval 1.5 to 3.0) and SOL at 1.2%, with an interval that includes zero.
Spikes fade within a day; the base level moves slowly. A gap halves in about 15 to 19 hours for BTC, ETH and SOL, and in 5 for HYPE against Binance. The best of three entry thresholds I tried waits for a settled 8-hour gap above 40% a year and leaves when it flips. It kept 1.2 to 2.8% a year after taker fees against Binance and 0.6 to 1.7% against Bybit. Lower thresholds lost money after fees. Holding the position the whole time earned about 7%, because the gap stayed on one side most of the time. That's slightly above the gap itself, since the position grew with price.
Holding still takes work. The two legs sit on different venues. In a rally the short leg on Hyperliquid loses margin while the long leg on Binance gains it, and you have to move collateral across before the short gets liquidated. I simulated both legs hour by hour. At 2x per leg, BTC needed 7 transfers in three and a half years and SOL needed 33. At 5x per leg, SOL got liquidated.
In 2026 the trade earns about what T-bills pay. At 2x per leg the return on capital equals the gap: 1 to 5% a year in 2026 (BTC 2.3%, ETH 3.8%, SOL 1.2%, HYPE 4.7%), against the 3.6 to 3.8% that 3-month US Treasury bills paid over the same months. BTC and SOL earned less than T-bills, ETH about the same, and HYPE more. The money was in late 2023 and 2024.

HYPE data starts in May 2025 on Binance, so its numbers cover a shorter stretch. For BTC and SOL the gap runs wider in volatile weeks: SOL's absolute gap averaged 8% a year in the calmest third of 8-hour blocks (ranked by trailing 7-day volatility) and 19% in the most volatile third. ETH shows no such pattern.

How I measured it

Putting three venues on one clock

Hyperliquid settled every 8 hours until June 2023 and every hour since. Binance moved HYPE to 4 hours and SOL briefly to 2 hours during the FTX collapse, before the period I compare. I read each payment's interval from the time since the venue's previous settlement and spread the payment evenly over those hours. Every venue then has a rate for every hour, and I compare the same hours on each side. Comparing Hyperliquid's latest hourly print against the last 8-hour CEX print would mix one hour against eight.

Costs

Base-tier fees: Hyperliquid 0.045% taker and 0.015% maker, Binance 0.05% and 0.02%, Bybit 0.055% and 0.02%. A taker round trip on both legs costs 20 to 25 bps for BTC, ETH and SOL and 30 to 32 bps for HYPE once I add slippage from order book snapshots at $100k per leg. The price gap between Hyperliquid and the CEXs on hourly closes has a standard deviation of 2 to 6 bps over the last seven months, small next to the fees.

Persistence and uncertainty

I fit the half-life on 8-hour blocks, because CEX rates sit flat inside each 8-hour window and hourly data would overstate persistence. The intervals come from a block bootstrap on daily means with 30-day blocks. The gap stays high or low for weeks at a time, and treating every day as independent would make the intervals look tighter than they are.

What this can't see

  • Fees for large accounts. A desk with maker rebates pays far less, and that changes the spike-trading numbers.
  • Wicks inside the hour. The margin simulation runs on Binance hourly closes, and real liquidations use each venue's mark price.
  • Transfer time and cost. I count collateral moves but assume each one arrives in time and costs nothing.
  • Collateral mismatch. Hyperliquid margin is USDC, Binance and Bybit margin here is USDT, and the collateral could earn yield elsewhere.
  • Capacity. Book depth comes from a handful of snapshots, and Hyperliquid's API shows only 20 levels per side.

This describes what funding did in the past. It is not a recommendation to trade it.

What's next

Stablecoin lending rates make a fairer benchmark than T-bills, since that is where the collateral would sit. I also want mark-price candles with highs and lows for the margin test, and a run of order book snapshots instead of a handful, to put a number on capacity.
