How much of the bitcoin ETF bid is a basis trade?

November 2026 · ETF flows from January 2024 to November 2026

US spot bitcoin ETFs took in about $55bn net between their launch in January 2024 and November 2026. Some buyers want bitcoin exposure. Others are hedge funds running a carry trade: buy ETF shares, short CME bitcoin futures against them, and collect the premium futures trade at over spot. That second group has no view on price, and it can leave as fast as it came. I wanted to know how big it is.

Terms used here
Spot ETF
An exchange-traded fund that holds bitcoin itself. IBIT, FBTC, GBTC and nine others in the US.
Flow
Money that enters a fund (creations) minus money that leaves it (redemptions) on a day. I convert it from dollars to BTC at that day's US close price.
CME futures
Bitcoin futures on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, the venue most US funds use. One standard contract is 5 BTC, one micro contract 0.1 BTC.
Basis
How far a futures price sits above spot, here annualised to a 90-day contract. At expiry the gap closes, so a buyer of spot who shorts the future locks it in.
Basis trade
Long spot (or an ETF), short futures of the same size. It earns the basis and carries almost no price risk.
COT / TFF
The CFTC's weekly Commitments of Traders report. Its Traders in Financial Futures version splits CME positions by type of holder, as of each Tuesday.
Leveraged funds
The CFTC category for hedge funds and commodity trading advisers. Basis traders land here.
Asset managers
The CFTC category for pension funds, mutual funds and similar long-only money.
Hedge ratio
BTC of new leveraged-fund net short per BTC of ETF inflow in the same week.
Pre-registration
Writing down hypotheses, data rules and pass lines, with a date, before running any test.
95% interval
The range of values for the true number that the data supports, at 95% confidence.
p-value
How often a result at least this strong would show up by chance if the hypothesis were false. I count below 0.05 as a pass.

What I found

About a quarter of the inflow came with a new CME short. In 2025 and 2026, for each BTC that flowed into the ETFs in a week, leveraged funds added 0.23 BTC of net short on CME (95% interval 0.17 to 0.29). In 2024 the figure was 0.19. ETF flow explains 27% of the week-to-week swings in that short. In the 8 weeks of the test period with inflows above 20,600 BTC, leveraged funds added about 4,700 BTC more short than in other weeks.
I expected the basis share to shrink. It grew. Before the test I wrote down that the hedge ratio would land between 0.2 and 0.5, and that it would fall below its 2024 level because the premium had compressed. The first call held. The second missed: 0.19 in 2024, 0.23 after. The two intervals overlap, so what the data shows is no sign of a decline.
A falling basis did not predict outflows. If the carry trade drives flows, a sharp drop in the futures premium should push basis traders out of the ETFs within days. It didn't show up. A drop in the basis over five days, with the price move held constant, told me nothing about flows over the next five (p = 0.40). My trigger for a sharp drop, fixed from 2024 data, fired on only 8 of 460 test days, because the basis moved less in 2025-26.
Flows chase price, and price follows flows. A strong bitcoin day comes before strong inflows the next day, which matches the usual story about ETF buyers. The reverse also holds: a big inflow day comes before a stronger bitcoin return the next day. Both links pass the test, so I can't say flows only follow price. My rule called that a fail, and I kept the rule.

How I measured it

Rules first, data second

I committed the hypotheses, variables, thresholds and pass lines to the repository before I ran any test. 2024 served only to set the thresholds; January 2025 to November 2026 is the test. The test wasn't blind: the basis unwind of early 2025 made headlines, and I saw the CFTC short totals while checking the data. What the advance commit buys is that I couldn't move the goalposts after seeing a result. One fix came after the first run, in a cross-check that compared CME and Binance prices taken hours apart. It changed none of the test numbers, and the plan's amendment log records it.

Putting flows on the CFTC clock

CFTC positions are a Tuesday snapshot. I summed daily ETF flows from Wednesday to Tuesday so each week of flow matches one change in positions. Flows come from Farside Investors in dollars per fund. I converted each day to BTC at the 4 pm New York price, so a rising bitcoin price doesn't inflate the later flows. The short side adds CME standard contracts (5 BTC) and micro contracts (0.1 BTC). Holidays appear in the flow table as empty rows, and I dropped them instead of counting them as zero.

A free stand-in for the CME basis

CME price history costs money, so the basis comes from Binance quarterly futures against Binance spot, interpolated to 90 days. Deribit's quarterlies track it closely (daily correlation 0.97). A rough CME series from Yahoo's front-month quote agrees by quarter only loosely (correlation 0.41). The basis test is really a test of the offshore premium.

Tests

The hedge ratio is a regression of the weekly change in leveraged-fund net short on weekly ETF flow, with standard errors that allow for runs of similar weeks. The basis test regresses the next five days of flow on the past five-day basis change, holding the past five-day return fixed, since basis and price tend to fall together. The direction test compares two daily regressions with five lags each: flow on past returns, and return on past flows.

What this can't see

  • Who holds the short. The CFTC reports categories, not trades. A leveraged-fund short can hedge an ETF, hedge bitcoin held elsewhere, or bet on a fall. If no other leveraged-fund shorts rise with inflows, 0.23 overstates the basis share. If some basis traders file as asset managers or dealers, it understates it.
  • Basis trades on other venues. A fund can hedge on Binance or Deribit instead of CME, and those positions never reach the CFTC.
  • The CME premium itself. My basis series is offshore, and the CME premium can differ.
  • Why flows lead price by a day. With cash creations, an issuer can buy the bitcoin after the day the flow is booked, which could produce the link on its own. I haven't tested that.

This describes past flows and positions. It is not a recommendation to trade them.

What's next

I want to test whether the next-day link from flows to price is settlement timing, by matching each fund's creation schedule to intraday prices. Splitting flows by issuer could also help, since basis traders likely favour the cheapest and most liquid fund.
